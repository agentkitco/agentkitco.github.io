Tabletop RPG pack #16

NPC on demand

When to use: The players just asked a stranger a question and you have nothing.

Create an NPC for [SITUATION, e.g. "a night-shift ferry operator at a small river crossing"] in a [SETTING] game.
Give me: name, how they look in one detail, how they talk (a speech tic or phrase), what they want right now, what they're afraid of, one thing they know that the party would love to know, and what they'd want in exchange. Make the name fit the culture: [CULTURE / REGION].
Keep it to 80 words so I can read it in 30 seconds.
Tabletop RPG pack #21

Mystery with a clue web

When to use: You want a mystery that can't be derailed by one missed clue.

Design a mystery for [SETTING]. The central question is [WHO / WHAT / WHY]. Use the three-clue rule: for every key conclusion, include at least three different clues pointing to it.
Give: the truth (what really happened, in order), the suspects with motives, a clue list (each clue: where it's found, what it points to, and what happens if players miss it), 3 red herrings and a fallback if players get stuck.
Present the clue web as a table so I can check coverage.

Who it's for

Game masters, players who like to prep, and fiction writers building worlds. System-agnostic: it works for fantasy, sci-fi, horror or anything else, with or without a published rulebook.

Format and honest notes

PDF. Examples are illustrative and abridged, so your results will differ. The prompts never supply official rules or stats: check numbers against your own rulebook and the license of your system. Some tables and publishers have rules about AI use, so check yours.

Questions

Which tabletop systems does it work with?

Any of them. The prompts are system-agnostic and take a [SYSTEM] placeholder, so they work for fantasy, sci-fi, horror, narrative games or no system at all. Because AI can invent or misquote rules, the prompts tell it to label numbers as suggestions, and you check your own rulebook.

Is it okay to use AI at my table?

That is up to you and your group. Some players dislike AI, so raise it in session zero. The pack uses AI for brainstorming and prep while you make every creative decision at the table.

Is it only for game masters?

No. Fiction writers use the worldbuilding, culture, history, villain and consistency prompts as they are. Players can use the character hook and party glue prompts.
