AI Agent Examples: 5 Useful Agents for Work and Side Projects

Published 2026-10-15 · 3 min read · by AgentKit Co

"AI agent" gets used for everything from a chatbot with a nickname to a system that books flights on its own. For everyday work, the useful version is much simpler: a chat assistant with a clear job, written instructions, and a few rules about what it must never do. That's all a system prompt is, and you can set one up in ten minutes.

Here are five agents people actually need, what makes each one good, and a complete system prompt for the first one so you can try it now. (New to the idea? Start with what an AI agent is.)

What makes an agent useful instead of annoying

Good agents share four traits:

  1. One job. "Turn notes into action items" beats "be my assistant".
  2. Rules about uncertainty. If it doesn't know, it says so instead of guessing.
  3. A fixed output format. You can scan the answer in seconds.
  4. A human at the end. It drafts. You review, then send, post or submit.

1. Meeting notes to action items

Meetings produce decisions that nobody writes down. This agent turns raw notes into a short record, and its most important rule is the one most tools skip: it never invents an owner or a deadline.

ROLE
You are a careful meeting-notes assistant. You turn raw notes or transcripts into a clean record.

GOAL
Produce a summary that someone who missed the meeting can read in two minutes.

METHOD
1. Read all of the notes before writing.
2. Identify decisions (things agreed), action items (things someone will do), and open questions (things unresolved).
3. For each action item, find the owner and the due date in the notes. If either is not stated, write "owner: not stated" or "due: not stated". Never guess.
4. Separate facts from opinions. Do not add content that isn't in the notes.
5. If two parts of the notes conflict, list the conflict under "Needs clarification".

OUTPUT FORMAT
- Summary (max 5 lines)
- Decisions (bullets)
- Action items (table: task | owner | due | notes)
- Open questions (bullets)
- Needs clarification (bullets, or "None")

RULES
- Keep names exactly as written. Do not infer job titles.
- Do not editorialize. If the notes are too thin, say what's missing.
- End by asking whether I want a short follow-up email draft (draft only).

To test it, paste notes with no owners at all. A good agent writes "owner: not stated" for everything instead of making up names.

2. Customer reply drafter

For a small shop or a side business, replies take more time than they should. The agent drafts answers using only the policies you paste in, and flags anything it can't answer, such as refund exceptions, angry messages or legal issues, for a human. The rule that matters: it never promises a refund, a date or a discount that your policy doesn't state.

3. Study buddy

A good tutor quizzes you instead of lecturing. The study agent explains in short chunks, asks you a question after each, gives hints before answers, and ends with three things to review tomorrow. It also refuses to do graded assignments for you, which is the right call for your learning and for your school's rules.

4. Content repurposer

One article can become a week of posts, but only if the facts survive the trip. The agent's trick is a "facts to double-check" list at the end of every draft: every number and claim it used, so you can compare against the source in a minute.

5. Side project shipping coach

Side projects die from scope, not lack of ideas. A weekly check-in agent asks what you finished, compares it with your plan, cuts anything that doesn't fit your real hours, and keeps a "not now" list for good ideas that don't belong in version 1. It never promises results or income, because it can't know them.

How to test any agent before you rely on it

  • Write three inputs designed to trip it up: missing information, a request that breaks a rule, and a messy real example.
  • Score each answer on accuracy, rule-following, format and tone.
  • Change one line at a time and re-run the same inputs.
  • Add a rule for every real failure.

Keep a human in the loop

Anything sent, posted, paid or submitted gets your review first. Agents are drafting and thinking aids, not professional advice on money, law, health or careers.
